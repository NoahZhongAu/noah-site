# Milestone 4 report: Story timeline

Date: 2026-09-05. Branch `milestone/4-story`, eight commits on top of `main`. Plan approved by the owner before code; the one open decision (fireflies on phones) was settled at approval: none.

## Works

- **The story section (PRD §4.2 as amended).** Seven steps from `content/`, sorted and formatted in the domain layer on the server; the client receives strings. Desktop from 768px: a sticky full-height backdrop with the seven era illustrations, the fireflies canvas, the dot rail and the "0N / 07" counter; each step is a full-height snap point. Under 768px: no sticky, no snap, no canvas; the steps are cards with a 16:9 `next/image` header from their era illustration. One DOM, CSS decides (PLAN §6 item 21).
- **Focus pull (ADR 0006).** The active layer is chosen by CSS from `data-era` on the section: outgoing layers go to `blur(18px) brightness(0.55) scale(1.06)` and opacity 0 over 1.1s, the incoming one resolves from that state. The push-in (`scale(1)` to `scale(1.07)` over 14s, linear, `forwards`) starts after the focus pull lands so the two never fight over `transform`, and restarts on every activation. Entry text (date, title, organisation, bullets) focuses in from opacity 0, `blur(8px)`, `translateY(8px)` over 0.7s with a 100ms stagger. Every number is a token. Only `transform`, `opacity` and `filter` animate.
- **Scroll snap, desktop only.** `scroll-snap-type: y mandatory` applies to `<html>` only while `data-snap` is set, which `StoryTimeline` does while any step is at least 60% visible. The cover and the Projects section carry `scroll-snap-align: start` too, so a wheel gesture out of step 1 or step 7 lands on a whole screen instead of being yanked back. Reduced motion and widths under 768px never snap. The cover's nav-anchor e2e passes with snap on.
- **Observer, no scroll listeners.** `useActiveStep` runs one `IntersectionObserver` at threshold 0.6 and returns the active index, which steps have been shown, and whether any step is on screen. `data-era` and `data-step` on the section are the contract for CSS and the tests.
- **Entry text fires once.** A shown step stays shown, so text does not blur out while a step scrolls away and does not re-fire on the way back (PRD §4.2 "once"; the demo re-fires, the PRD wins).
- **No JavaScript and pre-hydration.** The server renders every step shown; hydration narrows that to step 1 plus whatever the observer reports. Hiding is instant because the transition lives on the shown state only, so there is no fade-out flash on off-screen text at load. The no-JS e2e reads every entry at its final state with era 1 visible.
- **Fireflies (ADR 0005).** `Fireflies.tsx` mounts a canvas only on the client, only from 768px, only with motion allowed and Save-Data off. 24 particles from 768px, 10 below (which in practice never renders, see Does not work). Exactly a quarter are the big kind. Colour comes from the `--firefly` token read off the canvas at mount, never a literal in the component. The field logic is a pure module, `firefliesField.ts`, with six unit tests; the component has two more covering the three "no canvas" conditions and the on-screen, tab-hidden and off-screen pausing. Adds 2.5KB gzipped: first-party JS on `/` is 146.6KB against the 200KB budget.
- **Chrome.** Rail dots at `--fg-28`, the active one `--fg`, scaled 1.5× with a `--glow` shadow. Counter in mono, tabular figures. The `h2` "Story" and the section rule sit in a zero-height sticky header above the backdrop on desktop and in flow on phones, so the heading order is one `h1`, then `h2` per section, then `h3` per entry.
- **Section rule draws.** `SectionRule` and `useOnceInView` are in; the rule draws once at a quarter visible. This exposed a milestone 1 bug: `EdgeGradientDefs` set `stop-color` as an attribute with `var(--fg)`, which SVG attributes do not resolve, so the stroke was black on the dark ground and invisible. It is a style now.
- **Reduced motion.** No snap (`scroll-snap-type` computes to `none`), no blur, no push-in, no canvas, every step at its final state, era switches by instant opacity. A specificity gap surfaced in the e2e: the active-layer selector outranked the reduced-motion override, so the active era kept a `blur(0)` filter and a held push-in scale. The override now carries a matching selector.
- **Gates.** `pnpm check` green: 58 unit tests in 16 files (up from 44). `pnpm e2e` green: 28 tests, 10 new in `story.spec.ts` covering height and sticky, era, counter and rail per step, snap presence inside and absence outside the story, text focus once, layer blur state, canvas pause off-screen, axe mid-story, phone stacking with no sticky, no canvas and no 1920px layer request, Save-Data, reduced motion, and no JavaScript.
- **Lighthouse mobile, local, three runs on the final tree:** performance 0.97, 0.97, 0.97 with LCP 2.6s, 2.6s, 2.7s; accessibility, best practices and SEO 1.00 on every run. Unchanged from the end of milestone 3, which took work: see the first item under Assumed.
- Screenshots beside this report: `milestone-4-story-desktop.jpg` (step 1), `milestone-4-story-transition.jpg` (mid focus pull into step 4), `milestone-4-story-step4.jpg`, `milestone-4-story-phone.jpg`.

## Does not work

- **The frame-time gate could not be measured on the profile ADR 0006 names.** `pnpm frame-time` drives a 1080×1920 viewport at 4× CPU throttle through one transition with fireflies running, three times, and reports the best average. Results on this Mac:

  | Chromium | CPU | blur | fireflies | average fps |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | headless (software raster) | 4× | 18px | on | 15.8 |
  | headless | 4× | 10px | on | 15.6 |
  | headless | 4× | 18px | off | 16.2 |
  | headless | 4× | none | on | 22.5 |
  | headless | 4× | none | off | 23.5 |
  | headless | 1× | 18px | on | 17.0 |
  | headed (GPU compositor) | 4× | 18px | on | 60.0 |
  | headed | 1× | 18px | on | 60.0 |

  Headless Chromium rasterises in software, so the blur radius makes no difference (18px, 10px and none are within 7fps of each other) and the page sits near 15fps even with no throttle; that is a no-GPU profile, not a phone. Headed Chromium composites on the Mac's GPU, which is far stronger than a mid-range Android's, and pins 60fps. **ADR 0006 says drop blur to 10px if the number is under 50fps. I did not, because the only sub-50 numbers come from the profile where 10px changes nothing and even 0px stays under 25fps.** The script now defaults to the GPU run. The honest state: the gate is unmeasured on real mid-range hardware. See Unsure about.
- **Seven illustrations load on desktop as soon as the story is near.** The layers are `next/image` lazy with `fetchPriority="low"`, but Chrome's lazy margin is generous, so all seven 1920px variants (about 1MB) are requested shortly after the cover paints on a desktop viewport. They are below the fold, desktop is not the Lighthouse profile, and the phone never requests them (the backdrop is `display: none`; the e2e asserts no 1920px request at 390px). Deferring the six inactive layers until the story is on screen is the same trick the card headers now use and is a small follow-up.
- **The 10-particle mobile count never renders.** It is in `particleCount` and tested, but the canvas is gated to 768px and up, so the only way to see 10 particles is a desktop-layout viewport narrower than 768px, which the layout does not produce. Kept because the PRD text names it.
- **The 16:9 phone header is the full 1920×1080 illustration served at 640px.** No portrait crop exists (PRD §12 does not ask for one); at 390px the figure is small.
- `TimelineRail` and `TimelineCounter` are `aria-hidden`, so a screen reader gets no "3 of 7". The step list is the structure; the visual chrome restates it. Milestone 7's accessibility pass can decide whether the counter should be live.

## Assumed

- **Card header images are not in the server HTML.** The first Lighthouse runs with the story in place scored 0.95, 0.95, 0.96 with LCP at 2.9s, against 0.97 and 2.6s at the end of milestone 3. `fetchPriority="low"` changed nothing. A build with the phone headers removed went straight back to 0.97 and 2.6s, so the cause was the first four lazy headers (48KB at 750px) being requested inside the window where the cover's fonts are still arriving on the simulated 1.6Mbps link. Now `useActiveStep` runs a second observer with a one-viewport margin and a header mounts only when its card is near; a `<noscript>` copy carries the image for visitors without JavaScript, and the no-JS e2e counts all seven. Cost: the header of a card pops in about a viewport before it is reached, which is not visible in practice, and the mobile headers depend on hydration for JS visitors.
- **Snap points on the cover and on Projects.** ADR 0006 asked milestone 4 to confirm that nav anchors still land with snap on. They did not: with mandatory snap on `<html>` and step 1 the only snap point near the cover, scrolling the cover's nav into view got yanked back to step 1, and Playwright's click never stabilised. A real reader wheeling up out of step 1 would hit the same thing. The cover and the first section after the story now have `scroll-snap-align: start` (a `.snap-start` class), so leaving the story snaps to a whole screen, and once no step is 60% visible the attribute drops and scrolling is free. Milestone 5's Projects section must keep `.snap-start`.
- **`data-snap` is set on `<html>` from any width.** The attribute means "a step is on screen"; CSS applies snap only at 768px and up and only without reduced motion. The tests assert on computed `scroll-snap-type`, not on the attribute.
- **The `h2` lives in a zero-height sticky header** rather than a heading block above the timeline, because a non-snapping block before a mandatory-snap section is skipped or yanked. On phones the same header is in flow above the first card.
- **`--dur-era` changed from 900ms to 1100ms** and eleven tokens were added for the transition, chrome and fireflies. All are listed on the styleguide.
- **`--measure-step` (60ch) and `--gutter-story`** are tokens now, as the milestone 2 report asked; `--text-step` is the step title size from the demo.
- **The fireflies' three gradient stops use one colour at three alphas.** The demo shifts hue slightly between stops (255,210,122 → 255,190,90 → 255,180,80). One token was the rule; the difference is not visible at 2 to 5px.
- **The canvas is CSS-pixel sized, not device-pixel.** Same as the demo; a halo is a gradient, so it does not look aliased at 2×. Sizing to `devicePixelRatio` would double the fill cost on phones for nothing.
- **Big particles are every fourth by index**, not 25% by chance, so the count is exact and testable.
- **Step 1 is always shown.** It is the server's initial seen set, so the first screen of the story never animates its text in. Every later step does.
- **The two decorative images use `next/image` without `preload`**, as the PRD's LCP element is the cover poster.
- `scripts/frame-time.mjs` is a hand-run script (`pnpm frame-time`), not a CI gate: the numbers vary by machine and the CI runner has no GPU.
- The scrim under the story is the demo's left-to-right darkening plus the cover's top and bottom scrim, as two new tokens (`--scrim-side`, `--scrim-side-mid`), in the (0, 20, 35) family the cover already uses rather than the demo's (0, 14, 28).

## Unsure about

- **Real-device frame time.** The one number that matters, a mid-range Android running the transition with two blurred layers and the canvas, does not exist. Chrome DevTools remote debugging on a real phone, or the same script against a BrowserStack device, would settle it in ten minutes. Until then ADR 0006's "reduce blur to 10px" rule has not been triggered, and the owner should decide whether to apply it pre-emptively.
- **Trackpad flings and mandatory snap.** With a mouse wheel each gesture moves one step. A fast trackpad fling can pass several snap points before Chrome's directional snap settles, so "one entry per gesture" is approximate. `scroll-snap-stop: always` on each step enforces it; not added, per the plan, until the owner has felt it.
- **Snap when the story is entered by nav anchor.** "Know more" and the Story link land on step 1 by `scrollIntoView`, then the observer sets `data-snap`, which re-snaps to step 1 in place. Works in Chromium; not verified in Safari or Firefox, whose re-snap-on-change timing differs.
- **The reviewer's notes, none blocking.** (1) A component-level fireflies test was promised in the plan and missing; added. (2) The rail dot's 6px and 12px were the only new story values not tokenised; `--rail-dot` and `--rail-glow` now. The bullet marker's 9×1px in `globals.css` predates this and stays. (3) `getWanted` reads Save-Data once per snapshot and does not subscribe to `connection` change events, so toggling Save-Data mid-session does not unmount the canvas until something else re-renders; ADR 0005 asks for the initial decision only. (4) Between hydration and the observer's first callback there is one commit in which only step 1 is shown; a step restored mid-story by scroll position is hidden for that commit and then shown. The unit test exercises the order; not seen in the browser.
- **The e2e `networkidle` waits and the image optimiser.** The first e2e run after the layers landed timed out four `goto` calls at `networkidle`. It did not reproduce: a cold `.next/cache/images` reaches idle in 0.9s on desktop and 0.6s on phone. The suspect is the M3 report's port-3000 trap (`reuseExistingServer` against a server still shutting down). If it shows in CI, the cache is cold there and the seven 1920px optimisations happen on the first desktop test.
- **Safari.** `scroll-snap-type` on `<html>` toggled by attribute, `filter: blur()` transitions on two full-screen layers, and `useSyncExternalStore` over `matchMedia` are the three things most likely to behave differently. Chromium only, as before.
- **Whether the era layers should mount lazily by state** (active ±1) instead of relying on `loading="lazy"`. It would cut the desktop request burst from seven images to three and is a small change to `EraBackdrop`, but it moves image presence into React state, which the "CSS reads data-era" design deliberately avoids.
